My First view

This is a short account of how I became interested in microcontrollers and the different ones I learned to use throughout college.

My First Experience

I was not introduced to microcontrollers until my first year of college. I had seen an Arduino once during school, but it seemed almost like something from another world. No one around me had even seen a breadboard before, so I never had the opportunity to understand how it worked.

We were never exposed to microcontrollers in school, so the entire subject felt mysterious to me. My school life could be summarized as: “Memorize as much as possible. There is no need to understand the meaning or value of what you learn—just remember it.” Knowing a lot was often seen as the best way to appear special or different in a world where everything seemed ordinary.

My Second Semester and the ESP8266

During my first semester of college, I was running a local media server on my laptop using Sonarr. I wanted to move it to lower-powered hardware so that I would not have to keep my laptop running all the time. That was when I came across a Reddit post about running similar services on an ESP8266, and I decided to give it a try.

Around my second semester, close to my CAT 2 exams, I bought an ESP8266, along with an OLED display and an accelerometer. At first, I wanted to build a controller for a platform game I used to play. However, learning the Arduino programming environment was exhausting, and I struggled to find enough free time between projects, reports, records, and studying.

It was not until my second year, during a holiday, that I finally had enough time to learn more about GitHub, explore the ESP8266 properly, and begin experimenting with it.

Other Things That Shaped My Interest

My family bought our first smartphone when I was eleven. From that day onward, I enjoyed exploring its settings, experimenting with different options, and learning how everything worked. Although I was introduced to the internet relatively late, I learned a great deal about cybersecurity and privacy through my old laptop.

My first laptop was a government-issued computer that came with BOSS OS and Windows in a dual-boot setup. It was constantly affected by viruses that could even corrupt any pendrive connected to it. The battery was damaged from the beginning, so I had to learn as much as I could to repair and maintain it.

I have always enjoyed learning something new with the internet as my reference. In the end, every microcontroller I worked with felt simple once I had its datasheet in front of me. That curiosity and willingness to experiment are what kept me interested in learning more.
